Project Plan
Barrier CENIC: Robust Simulation of Thin Objects in the Wild
________________


1. Summary
The Problem
Modern contact simulation methods struggle with thin objects and codimensional geometry (e.g., wires, thin sheets, surfaces with boundary).
In robotics-relevant scenes—bins, racks, utensils, clips, sheet metal parts—objects are often:
* Extremely thin relative to scene scale (sub-millimeter thickness)
* Open surfaces (not closed volumes)
* In sustained, sliding, or grazing contact
* In dense clutter
Existing methods typically fall into one of two categories:
1. Volumetric hydroelastic contact
   * Robust for thick, watertight solids
   * Cannot naturally represent infinitely thin or co-dimensional objects
   * Requires artificial thickening of geometry
2. Barrier-based rigid or deformable contact (e.g., IPC, ABD)
   * Very robust non-penetration guarantees
   * Often expensive
   * Can suffer conditioning challenges in large clutter scenes
   * Typically designed for different modeling assumptions
This leaves a gap: robust, well-conditioned, non-penetrating simulation of thin and codimensional objects in robotics-scale clutter scenes.
________________


2. Proposed Solution
We propose bCENIC (barrier CENIC) — a thin-object extension of the CENIC framework.
Core Idea
Instead of representing thin objects as full volumes, we construct:
* A thin volumetric layer (δ ≈ 0.1–1mm) around a mesh
* An extent field ( e(x) in [0,1] ):
   * 0 at the outer boundary
   * 1 at the original mesh
* A nonlinear hydroelastic-style pressure law
p(x) = E e(x) / (1 - e(x))

As a contact surface approaches the inner boundary (e → 1), the pressure blows up, preventing penetration.
Key Innovations
   1. Works for:
   * Closed watertight meshes
   * Co-dimensional surfaces (meshes with boundary)
   * Infinitely thin objects
   2. Nonlinear hydroelastic pressure with guaranteed blow-up near penetration
   3. Analytic continuation of the barrier function
→ Ensures smooth Newton solves over velocity search
   4. Novel β-regularization
      * Single dimensionless parameter β
      * Controls max stiffness of contact constraints
      * Ensures good conditioning
      * Stiffness cap scales as:
(m 4π^2) / (dt β)^2
      * As dt → 0, original barrier behavior is recovered
         5. End-of-step linear CCD guard
         * Ensures non-penetration at accepted states
         * May miss tiny mid-step passthroughs (to be quantified)
________________


3. Benefits
Practical Benefits
         * Handles thin wires, sheet metal, racks, clips, etc.
         * Eliminates need for artificial thickening
         * Guarantees non-penetration of inner mesh
         * Maintains good numerical conditioning
         * Scales to cluttered robotics scenes
Research Contributions
         * Thin-layer hydroelastic barrier formulation that is continuous
         * Codimension-capable contact model
         * Single-parameter conditioning regularization
         * Quantified tradeoff between stiffness and timestep
         * Empirical study of linear CCD error under rotation
________________


4. Known Drawbacks / Risks
         1. Additional volumetric layer construction
         * Requires offset meshing and tetrahedralization
         * Preprocessing cost
         2. Linear CCD approximation
         * May miss small rotational passthroughs mid-step
         * Needs mathematical error bound and empirical validation
         3. Nonlinear stiffness near boundary
         * Poor parameter choice (β too small) can cause conditioning issues, excessive timesteps
         4. Complexity vs simpler rigid contact models
         * More complex than simple constraint methods using mesh feature pairs (vertex-face, edge-edge).
________________


5. Scientific Objectives
The paper and project aim to:
         1. Quantify numerical robustness
         2. Measure conditioning behavior vs β
         3. Measure solver iterations and scalability
         4. Compare to state-of-the-art methods (ABD, IPC variants, volumetric hydroelastic)
         5. Quantify linear CCD approximation error
         6. Demonstrate real robotics-relevant capability
________________


6. Evaluation Strategy
Each demo produces:
         * 1–2 visual figures
         * 2–3 quantitative plots
         * Standardized instrumentation metrics
We target ~20–25 figures total (can prune later).
________________


7. Standard Metrics Across All Experiments
For every simulation:
Integrator Metrics
         * Accepted timestep dt
         * Rejected step count
         * Newton iterations 
         * Line search iterations
         * Real-time ratio (RTR)
         * Runtime breakdown (broadphase, contact assembly, solve, CCD)
Contact Metrics
         * active constraints (mean / max)
         * Min gap to inner boundary (1 − e)
         * Max pressure
         * Max contact force
         * Post-step CCD triggers
Conditioning Metrics
         * Solve iterations
         * Residual convergence
         * Condition number

8. Simulation Test Suite
We propose five demos.
________________


Demo 1 — Controlled Barrier & Conditioning Benchmark
Goal
Isolate numerical behavior in a simple setting.
Setup
Simple contact:
            * Thin plate vs rigid wedge
or
            * Sphere vs thin shell
Studies
               * β sweep
               * Accuracy sweep
               * With vs without analytic continuation
Figures
               1. Barrier function & continuation plot
               2. Newton convergence behavior
               3. Conditioning vs β
               4. Pressure vs approach-to-boundary
Outcome
Demonstrates:
               * Smooth solver behavior
               * Controlled stiffness
               * Proper blow-up behavior
________________


Demo 2 — Thin Objects in the Wild (Cluttered Scene)
Goal
Show real robotics relevance.
Setup
               * Thin-walled bin
               * Wire rack
               * Mixed thin objects dumped or manipulated
Studies
               * β sweep
               * Accuracy sweep
Figures
               1. Keyframes
               2. dt vs time
               3. #contacts vs time
               4. Runtime breakdown
Outcome
Shows:
               * Scalability
               * Robustness in clutter
               * No interpenetration of thin geometry
________________


Demo 3 — Large Clutter Numerical Characterization
Goal
Stress solver scaling.
Setup
50–200 objects with thin components.
Studies
Systematic parameter sweeps.
Figures
               1. Iterations vs β
               2. Conditioning vs β
               3. Runtime vs #contacts
               4. Rejected step statistics
Outcome
Clear quantitative performance characterization.
________________


Demo 4 — Nut & Bolt (Comparison to ABD)
Goal
Head-to-head comparison.
Setup
Threaded engagement with friction.
Metrics
               * Convergence
               * Stability
               * Runtime
               * Success rate
Figures
               1. Assembly progression
               2. dt vs time
               3. Solver iteration histogram
               4. Runtime per simulated second
Outcome
Demonstrates competitiveness in rigid-like precision tasks.
________________


Demo 5 — Rotational CCD Error Study
Goal
Quantify linear CCD approximation error.
Setup
High angular velocity near-grazing configurations.
Study
Sweep:
               * Angular velocity
               * Timestep size
Figures
               1. Geometric illustration
               2. False-negative heatmap (ω vs dt)
               3. Minimum separation margin vs dt
               4. Additional cost due to safeguard
Outcome
Converts a potential weakness into a quantified engineering tradeoff.
