What every demo should output (standardized instrumentation)
Think of each demo as producing:
* 2 “hero” visuals (keyframes or time-lapse) + ~2 quantitative figures.
* A shared stats table row (so you can do one big summary table across all demos).
Always log these signals per accepted step
Step control / integrator
* accepted dt, rejected-step count, LTE estimate (or your accuracy proxy), target accuracy
* #Newton iterations, #line search iterations (if any), termination reason
* runtime breakdown: broadphase, narrowphase, contact assembly, solve, post-check CCD
* total simulation wall time, real-time ratio (RTR)
Contact / barrier
* #active constraints (mean / max), constraint type counts (V–F, E–E, E–F, etc.)
* min distance / min “extent gap” to inner boundary (how close you got to e(x)=1)
* max / percentile(95/99) contact pressure, max contact force magnitude
* β used, computed “capped stiffness” (your implied max stiffness ∝ 1/(β·dt)²)
Linear algebra / conditioning
* KKT/system matrix condition number estimate (or practical proxy: PCG iterations, residual history)
* solve iterations and final residual
* (optional but very compelling) smallest eigenvalue / spectral estimate for the contact Hessian block
Correctness / robustness
* end-of-step nonpenetration status (pass/fail)
* #post-check CCD “fixups” triggered
* max interpenetration detected (should be 0 for guaranteed quantities; report tolerance)
Standard ablation sweeps (use across all demos, but don’t plot all for all)
* β sweep: e.g., {0.25, 0.5, 1, 2, 4} (dimensionless)
* accuracy sweep: 2–3 target accuracies (loose/medium/tight)
* initial velocity sweep: slow/medium/fast (to trigger step rejection + CCD edge cases)

Proposed 5-demo suite (covers your goals better than 4)
You can cut to 4 later, but building 5 now gives you room to prune, and makes it harder for reviewers to poke holes.
Demo 1 — Controlled “Barrier & Continuation” microbenchmark (geometry-simple)
Objective: isolate your barrier pressure discretization + analytic continuation + β-conditioning in a scene with almost no confounds.
Setup ideas (pick one):
* Rotating thin plate vs sharp wedge (codim plate if you want), or
* Sphere vs thin shell (two-sided contact) with a known contact normal field.
Ablations:
* β sweep × 2 accuracies × 2 speeds.
* Also compare “no continuation” vs “with continuation” (even if “no continuation” is just for analysis).
Figures (~4):
1. Barrier function plot: your p(e)=E·e/(1−e) and its analytic continuation branch; overlay several β values (show C¹ continuity at the splice).
2. Newton convergence: iteration count or residual vs iteration for representative steps near stiff contact (with/without continuation).
3. Conditioning vs β: cond-proxy (PCG iters) and/or Newton iterations.
4. Max pressure vs min(1−e): verify controlled approach to the inner boundary without penetration; show stability.
Why this matters: This is where you “prove” the math choices actually fix solver pathologies, before you throw clutter at it.
________________


Demo 2 — Codimension “thin objects in the wild”: wire rack + thin-walled bin dump (CENIC-style)
Objective: make the codim claim unavoidable and match the motivating scenario CENIC itself uses (thin bin + thin wires). (ar5iv)
Setup:
* A bin with thin walls + a wire dish rack (codim rods/wires), dump mixed objects in.
* Include at least one “infinitely thin” surface-with-boundary (e.g., sheet metal cutout) to force codim handling.
Compare:
* bCENIC vs baseline CENIC ICF/hydroelastic variant (if you have it), plus one “rigid-style” method if feasible.
* If you can’t run IPC here (too different), that’s okay—this is your capability demo.
Figures (~4):
1. Keyframes / montage (2–3 frames) highlighting thin contacts.
2. dt vs time with contact transitions annotated (dump impact, sliding, settling).
3. #contacts vs time and breakdown by constraint type.
4. Runtime breakdown pie/stack averaged over phases (impact vs settle).
Extra plot if you have room: “post-check CCD triggers vs time” to show it’s not constantly rescuing you.

Demo 3 — Cluttered manipulation-relevant pile (stress scalability + numerical characterization)
Objective: your original “clutter of objects in the wild,” but make it a numerical study, not just a pretty scene.
Setup:
* 50–200 objects, mixed mesh complexity (include thin parts: utensils, clips, brackets).
* Controlled “shake table” or robot end-effector pushing through.
Study design (important):
* Hold geometry fixed, vary β and accuracy.
* Make sure you hit: high contact count, lots of sliding, repeated impacts.
Figures (~4):
1. Keyframe(s) + zoom-in insets of worst contact regions.
2. Iterations vs β: Newton iters (mean/95%) and solve iters (mean/95%) as lines; separate curves per accuracy.
3. Conditioning vs β: cond-proxy / PCG iters and failure rate (if any) vs β.
4. Performance scaling: runtime per step vs #contacts (scatter), include fitted trend line.
Add a summary table row: mean dt, rejected-step rate, RTR, max #contacts.

Demo 4 — Nut & bolt (head-to-head with ABD + highlights discrete-vs-continuous trajectory issues)
Objective: a direct “rigid-ish” contact benchmark where ABD is known to shine and reviewers expect a comparison. ABD is explicitly motivated by issues in rigid-body IPC linearization and improves performance by relaxing exact rigidity and enabling piecewise-linear trajectories. (arXiv)
Setup:
* Threaded nut spinning onto bolt under torque, with realistic clearances.
* Include friction (static→dynamic transitions) and occasional jamming contact.
Compare:
* bCENIC vs ABD (and optionally a simpler baseline, if you have one).
Figures (~4):
1. Montage showing engagement + jam/slip events.
2. Torque/angle vs time (or progress along thread vs time): show physical plausibility.
3. Step size behavior: dt vs time; show you don’t collapse dt unnecessarily.
4. Solver cost: runtime per simulated second + iteration histograms.
Key metrics:
* success rate across initial misalignments (robustness)
* penetration = 0 (or tolerance) across entire run
________________


Demo 5 — “Rotational CCD error” torture test (quantify your linear-CCD approximation risk)
Objective: you explicitly said you need to mathematically quantify linear CCD error for rotating rigid bodies. This demo becomes the empirical companion to that analysis and preempts reviewer attacks.
Setup:
* Two bodies with high angular velocity:
   * a thin ring rotating past a thin plate edge
   * or a spinning key-shaped object passing near a slotted bracket
* Choose initial configurations where nonlinear trajectory CCD would detect contact, but linearized CCD is borderline.
Protocol:
* Sweep angular velocity and dt.
* Measure when linear-CCD misses vs when your barrier still guarantees non-penetration at endpoints.
Figures (~4):
1. Schematic figure: true curved trajectory vs linear segment, defining the error measure you’ll bound.
2. False negative rate heatmap over (ω, dt) for linear CCD, plus region where endpoints remain safe.
3. “Minimum separation margin” vs dt: how close you get in worst cases.
4. Cost of safety: additional rejected steps / dt reduction vs ω.
Deliverable: This is where you justify “false negatives only for small passthroughs” with a quantified bound and an empirical phase diagram.

What to put in the paper as “Other plots not tied to cases”
These belong in a Method + Ablations section, not the demo section.
A. Analytic continuation (β family)
* Plot barrier energy/force derivatives across the splice point for multiple β.
* Show continuity (C⁰/C¹) and improved Newton stability (fewer line search stalls).
B. Quadrature / discretization choices (barycentric dual, etc.)
* A figure comparing:
   * naive quadrature points vs barycentric dual placement
   * resulting max pressure / force vs approach-to-boundary
* A “pathological triangle” example where naive discretization fails to capture blow-up in the right location.
C. β regularization law
* Plot max stiffness cap = C/(β·dt)² vs dt for a few β values (straight lines in log-log).
* Add a plot showing that as dt→0 you recover the original barrier behavior (numerically: peak pressure vs dt at fixed penetration margin).
